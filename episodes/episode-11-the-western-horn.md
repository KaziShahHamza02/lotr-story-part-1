# Episode 11: The Western Horn

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 641 (story only)

## Summary

On the wind-swept edge of the frozen plain, the western border-men overtake Mara’s company. In a tense parley at bow-point, Mara must test where their marshal’s loyalties lie before the red lances regroup behind them.

## Story

They were half a mile onto the frozen plain when the western horn blew behind them.

It was the same deep, brassy note Mara had heard in Vale Hollow’s square, echoing flat and loud across the ice. There were no boulders here, no pines, and no sunken walls—only wind-scoured snow crusted over grey stone.

Warden Lira raised her stave, and her twenty wardens formed a tight half-circle facing south, iron-shod ash staves braced against the ice and arrows laid across bowstrings.

Up from the pass rode thirty horsemen in grey wool cloaks, their iron-rimmed shields slung across their saddles. None of them drew steel as they approached, though their horses’ breath steamed like furnace smoke in the bitter air.

Twenty paces from the wardens’ line, their leader raised a gloved fist. The column halted.

He was broad-shouldered, with silver threading his dark beard and a heavy iron ring on his bridle hand. He looked past Lira’s bows straight at Mara.

"Mara Vale," he called over the wind. "I am Marshal Orin of the River Wardens. Elder Tomren told us you came north."

"He also told us not to trust a rider’s horn," Mara called back.

Orin’s mouth twitched beneath his beard. "Wise old man. Still swinging that village axe?"

"He saved our square," Bram shouted from behind Lira’s shoulder. "You drove the red lances out!"

"We did," Orin said, swinging down from his saddle and walking forward alone with both hands open at his sides. Lira’s bowmen tracked every step, yet he did not flinch until he stood five paces from the staves. "And we didn't ride three days through sleet to fight green banners on the ice. We came because the Watchtower in Vale Hollow wasn't the only lamp that woke."

He reached into his cloak and pulled out a small brass disc—not notched like Calder’s copper token, but engraved with a river-bend and a single star. "Every border-house along the west road saw white fire on the horizon. My orders are older than the red lances, Warden Lira: if the northern beacons burn, the River Wardens answer."

Lira did not lower her stave. "And what do you know of the glass gate?"

Orin’s eyes narrowed toward the dark spire rising at the far edge of the plain. "Only that three days ago, riders with red pennants began crossing the high ridges from the east—more than twenty, more than fifty. Whatever the scarred captain failed to choke at the Stone Hearth, his masters mean to finish at the tower."

Calder stepped forward beside Mara, his hand resting on the cracked Keeper’s slate inside his coat. "If you mean to help, Marshal, why chase us up the pass instead of holding the Hearth?"

"Because the Hearth is already burning," Orin replied evenly. "And your five green coats at the pillars told us you’d taken the needle onto the plain. Thirty horses can cross flat ice in two hours; on foot, you’ll be caught in the open before dusk."

Mara looked at Lira, then at Fen, then at the thirty silent riders waiting behind Orin. Every instinct told her to guard the needle—yet if fifty red lances were already gathering at the glass tower, twenty wardens on foot would never reach Halea in time.

She unwrapped one corner of her scarf, letting the pale gold glow of the needle touch the snow.

Orin did not reach for it. Instead, he dropped to one knee on the ice—and behind him, thirty River Wardens dismounted as one.

"The needle belongs to the road," Orin said quietly. "Not to me. Put the children behind our shields, Warden Lira, and let’s cross this plain together."

Lira lowered her stave an inch, looking at Mara. "Your call, mapmaker."

Mara wrapped the needle tight and looked toward the black-glass spire. "Mount up," she said. "Before the wind turns."

## Continuity notes

- The unidentified western force is led by **Marshal Orin** of the **River Wardens** (thirty horsemen in grey wool with iron-rimmed shields and brass discs engraved with a river-bend and star).
- Orin confirms that western border-houses saw the white fire and rode north under old standing orders to answer the beacons.
- Orin warns that more than fifty eastern riders with red pennants are converging on the black-glass tower.
- After a tense parley, Orin pledges his riders’ support without claiming the brass needle, uniting the River Wardens with Lira’s green-bannered company, Mara, Fen, Calder, and Bram to cross the Glass Plain.
