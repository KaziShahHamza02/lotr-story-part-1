# Episode 9: The Token on the Stone

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 695 (story only)

## Summary

Before descending from the pass onto the frozen plain, Mara and Fen reunite with Calder, who followed his copper token up from the Stone Hearth. He brings a Keeper’s slate—and the hidden history of why the Winter Road was sealed.

## Story

The wind at the top of the pass carried no snow, only fine ice needles that stung every inch of bare skin.

Warden Lira halted the company beside a half-buried stone shelter where three broken pillars leaned inward like ribs. Below them stretched a white plain so flat and pale it looked like frozen milk, ending miles away at a thin dark needle on the horizon.

"We rest an hour," Lira called down the line. "Check your boot-wraps before we step onto the ice."

Fen huddled inside his wool cloak behind the leeward wall. Mara sat beside him, keeping the wrapped brass needle tucked close. For the first time since Vale Hollow, she felt the weight of how far they had come—and how little they still knew about what waited ahead.

A whistle sounded from the pass behind them—two short notes, then one long.

Lira’s wardens turned as one, bows half-drawn. Up the switchback trudged two figures: one of the five green-cloaked wardens Lira had left at the Stone Hearth, and beside him a lean man in mud-caked grey, leaning heavily on a crooked ash branch.

"Calder!" Fen scrambled to his feet.

Mara hurried down the snow-packed track. Calder’s face was gaunt with cold, his boots split at the seams, and a fresh bruise darkened his cheekbone. Yet when he saw them, the corner of his mouth twitched upward.

He reached inside his coat and held out the copper token—the one Mara had left on the granite threshold of the Stone Hearth.

"You kept your promise," Calder said, his voice hoarse.

"And you lost the riders," Mara said, taking his elbow to steady him toward the shelter.

"Mostly." Calder sank onto a flat stone beside the wardens’ cold camp. "The marsh took two of their horses. By the time I doubled back to the Hearth, your fire was already burning white—and five of these green coats were waiting by the pillars."

He unbuckled a flat leather satchel slung under his arm and drew out a thin slate plaque, cracked down one corner and bound with copper wire. On its face, beneath a circle and three cuts, lines of neat script had been scratched into the stone.

Lira knelt beside him, eyeing the slate. "Where did you get that?"

"From the ferryman’s lockbox beneath the old ford," Calder said. "My father told me the copper token only opened the box. He never told me what was inside." He tapped the cracked slate. "The Keepers didn't close the Winter Road because of snow, Warden. They closed it because the black-glass tower isn't a watchhouse. It's a lock."

Mara leaned closer. "A lock on what?"

"Read the last line," Calder said.

Lira traced the scratched letters with a gloved finger and read aloud: *"Three branches feed the northern hearth; three keys hold the glass gate shut. Should the red lances break the plain, return the brass needle to Halea’s hand before winter unmakes the seal."*

Silence settled over the shelter, broken only by the hiss of ice-wind across the stone.

"Halea," Lira murmured. "My sister."

"And the three cuts on your token," Mara said, looking from the copper disc to the brass needle wrapped in her scarf. "The watchtower in Vale Hollow, the Stone Hearth, and the glass tower."

Calder nodded slowly. "The road wasn't built to carry wagons north. It was built to carry that needle back if the seal ever failed."

Fen looked out over the pale expanse of the frozen plain toward the distant dark spire. "Then why did the red lances try to stop us at the Hearth?"

"Because if the Hearth goes cold, no one can cross the pass," Lira said, rising to her feet and fastening her hood. "And if no one reaches Halea with the needle, the glass gate opens on its own."

Calder pushed himself up with his ash branch, wincing as weight settled on his bruised knee. "I came this far, mapmaker. Might as well see what’s at the end of my father’s road."

Mara smiled faintly, feeling the warmth of the needle against her ribs. For the first time since the three-sided milestone, the company was whole again.
