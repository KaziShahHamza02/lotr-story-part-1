# Episode 8: The Green Banners

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 708 (story only)

## Summary

North of the Stone Hearth, Mara and Fen meet the green-bannered company descending from the frozen ridge. Their leader reveals why they answered the Hearth’s white fire—and what has begun to stir beyond the glass plain.

## Story

Snow began an hour past the Stone Hearth, dry as salt and blowing sideways off the peaks.

Mara and Fen followed a sunken cart track lined with wind-scoured pines. Beneath her scarf, the brass needle stayed still and warm, pointing straight toward a narrow pass where two glacier-streaked ridges met.

Halfway up the pass, Fen stopped and pointed. "Look."

Through the blowing snow, a column of dark shapes was descending the switchbacks toward them. No horses. These travelers moved on foot in hooded coats of grey wool, each carrying a long ash stave shod with iron. Above the front rank snapped three narrow banners of deep pine-green, stitched with a pale silver circle.

Mara stepped off the track behind a boulder, pulling Fen with her. "Wait."

"They don't have red pennants," Fen whispered. "And they answered the Hearth."

Before Mara could reply, a horn—low and wooden, unlike the sharp brass of the eastern riders—sounded twice from the pass. A woman detached herself from the front rank and strode down the snow-packed track alone, her ash stave planting crisply into the crust.

She stopped ten paces from their boulder, lowered her hood, and looked straight at Mara's chest.

"The Stone Hearth hasn't burned white in twenty winters," the woman said. Her voice was calm, roughened by cold wind. "Show me the needle, mapmaker."

Mara stiffened. "How do you know what I am?"

"Because only a mapmaker's brass carries the spark from Vale Hollow," the woman answered. "I am Warden Lira of the Winter Gate. And if you came up from the Hearth, you ran straight through twenty red lances to get here."

Mara unwrapped the scarf an inch. Even in the blowing snow, the needle's tip glowed pale gold, steady as a hearth-coal.

Lira let out a long breath that turned to white mist. Behind her, the green-bannered company halted along the switchback, twenty strong, watching the valley below with drawn bows and quiet discipline.

"Then the scarred captain failed," Lira said.

"He tried to choke the tripod with pitch and salt-iron," Fen said, stepping out from behind the boulder. "We lit it first."

Lira's gaze softened for a fraction of a second as she looked at the boy, then sharpened again on Mara. "He won't stop at the Hearth. When the red lances couldn't reach us across the pass, they turned south to cut off the watchtowers one by one. We've been waiting on the ridge for six days, watching for white fire."

"Why didn't you come down before?" Mara asked.

"Because the Winter Road is sealed from the north unless the Hearth answers," Lira said, tapping her iron-shod stave against the frozen stone. "Our banners aren't an army, Mara Vale. We're a relief watch. Three weeks ago, the black-glass tower on the plain began to hum—the same way your road-vault did—and the ice around its base started melting upward."

Mara remembered the ember in Vale Hollow's watchtower: snow falling upward, green banners marching, and a woman's voice whispering her name.

"There was a voice in the ember," Mara said quietly. "From the glass tower. Who was she?"

Lira's hand tightened on her stave. For the first time, uncertainty crossed her weathered face. "My sister, Halea. She kept the inner watch at the glass tower until the eastern riders cut the plain last autumn. If her voice reached Vale Hollow, she's still alive—and she's holding the gate alone."

Fen looked back down the snowy pass toward the distant smoke-free glow of the Stone Hearth. "Then we're going back to Vale Hollow after?"

"Not yet," Lira said. She turned and raised two fingers to the column above. The green banners dipped once, and the wardens began descending the switchback in earnest. "Five of my company will march down to the Stone Hearth to hold the ring and watch for your ferryman—Calder, if he kept his word. The rest of us cross the pass tonight."

She looked at Mara. "The needle brought you this far. Will you carry it across the glass plain?"

Mara wrapped the warm brass back into her scarf. Behind them lay Vale Hollow, Elder Tomren, and a broken company; ahead lay frozen stone and a voice that knew her name.

"Show us the pass," Mara said.
